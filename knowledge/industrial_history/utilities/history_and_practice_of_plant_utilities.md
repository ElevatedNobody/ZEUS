## Utilities as a system {claim: historical_fact}
Early plants generated their own steam and power on site. Grid supply and large central
utility blocks changed the economics but not the dependency: process units cannot run
without steam, cooling water, instrument air and power, so utilities became the single
largest common-cause failure risk on a site.

## Cooling water {claim: historical_fact}
Once-through river and sea water cooling gave way to recirculating cooling towers as
environmental discharge limits tightened. Cooling tower chemistry - scaling, corrosion,
biological growth including Legionella control - became a discipline in its own right.

## Compressed air {claim: historical_fact}
Instrument air replaced steam and hydraulic actuation for most control functions.
Because pneumatic actuators are designed to fail to a safe position on air loss, the
instrument air system became a safety-related utility and is normally provided with
dedicated drying, receivers and backup.

## Steam systems {claim: engineering_principle}
Steam is generated at high pressure, let down through turbines where work can be
extracted, and used at several pressure levels. Balancing the steam header network -
generation, extraction, let-down and venting - is a continuous optimisation, and venting
steam to atmosphere is the visible sign that the balance is wrong.

## Steam traps {claim: engineering_principle}
Failed-open steam traps waste steam continuously and silently. Trap surveys with
ultrasonic or thermal methods typically find a significant failed percentage in plants
without a programme, and their repair is one of the fastest-payback energy measures
available.

## Water treatment {claim: engineering_principle}
Boiler feed water requires demineralisation and deaeration; cooling water requires
scale, corrosion and biological control; effluent requires treatment before discharge.
Each is a chemistry control loop whose failure appears later as equipment damage rather
than as an immediate process upset.

## Common-cause failure {claim: engineering_principle}
A utility failure trips every unit that depends on it simultaneously. Power dips,
instrument air loss and cooling water loss are therefore analysed as site-wide scenarios
with their own emergency procedures, not as local equipment failures.

## Nitrogen and flare {claim: engineering_principle}
Nitrogen for purging and blanketing and the flare system for safe disposal of relieved
hydrocarbon are safety utilities. Flare header integrity, knockout drum level, and
liquid carryover to the flare tip are recurring items in process safety audits.

## Energy management {claim: modern_best_practice}
Utility metering at unit level, steam balance monitoring, cooling tower approach
tracking, compressed air leak surveys and power factor management form the practical
core of an industrial energy programme; ISO 50001 provides the management framework.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Modern sites treat utilities as an asset class with their own criticality ranking,
redundancy analysis, and preventive maintenance - because the reliability of the whole
site is bounded by them.
