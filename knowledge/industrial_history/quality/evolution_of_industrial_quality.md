## Inspection-based quality {claim: historical_fact}
Early quality control meant final inspection: make the parts, then sort the good from
the bad. It detected defects but did nothing to prevent them and made quality a cost
rather than a capability.

## Statistical process control {claim: historical_fact}
Walter Shewhart's control chart, developed at Bell Telephone Laboratories in 1924,
distinguished ordinary process variation from assignable causes, allowing intervention
only when it was justified. Deming and Juran extended the approach and were highly
influential in post-war Japanese industry.

## Total quality and continuous improvement {claim: historical_fact}
Company-wide quality control, quality circles, and kaizen spread from Japan through the
1960s to 1980s. Motorola introduced Six Sigma in 1986 as a defect-reduction methodology,
later combined with lean thinking.

## Quality management standards {claim: historical_fact}
ISO 9001 was first published in 1987, based on earlier British standard BS 5750, and
established third-party certification of management systems. Sector variants followed for
automotive, aerospace and other industries.

## Quality in process plants {claim: engineering_principle}
In continuous process industries the product is a stream, not a part. Quality control
means keeping properties within specification through analysers, laboratory sampling and
blending, and the equivalent of a defect is an off-specification batch or a giveaway -
producing better than specification and losing the margin.

## Measurement systems {claim: engineering_principle}
No quality decision is better than the measurement behind it. Laboratory method
precision, analyser calibration and sampling representativeness determine whether a
result reflects the process or the sampling error, which is why measurement system
analysis precedes any capability study.

## Certificates and traceability {claim: engineering_principle}
For engineering materials, the quality record is the material test certificate and the
traceability chain from heat number to installed component. Positive material
identification exists because a mis-delivered or mis-installed alloy has caused fatal
high-temperature failures.

## Welding and fabrication quality {claim: engineering_principle}
Fabrication quality is controlled through qualified procedures, qualified welders,
defined NDT coverage and documented heat treatment. The inspection and test plan is the
contractual record of what was verified and by whom.

## Quality of maintenance work {claim: engineering_principle}
Maintenance quality is measured by how long a repair lasts. Precision practices -
alignment tolerance, torque control, cleanliness, correct spare - are the direct quality
controls on maintenance work, and rework rate is its defect metric.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current practice integrates quality with reliability: control charts on key process
variables, defect elimination for repeat failures, supplier quality requirements written
into purchase specifications, and audit against both the management system and its actual
field application.
