## From coal chemistry to petrochemistry {claim: historical_fact}
Organic chemicals were originally derived from coal tar. From the 1920s in the United
States, refinery off-gases became a cheaper source of olefins, and petrochemistry
displaced coal chemistry for most bulk products.

## Early landmark products {claim: historical_fact}
Isopropanol from refinery propylene (1920) is generally cited as the first commercial
petrochemical. Polyethylene was discovered accidentally at ICI in 1933 and produced
commercially from 1939; nylon was announced by DuPont in 1938; polypropylene followed
Ziegler and Natta's catalyst work in the mid-1950s.

## Steam cracking {claim: historical_fact}
Steam cracking of ethane, propane, naphtha or gas oil became the central process of the
industry after the Second World War. Feedstock choice is regional: ethane-based crackers
dominate where cheap gas liquids exist, naphtha crackers where refineries supply the
feed and a wider slate of co-products is wanted.

## Aromatics {claim: historical_fact}
Benzene, toluene and xylenes were originally recovered from coke ovens and later from
catalytic reformate and pyrolysis gasoline. Paraxylene for polyester and benzene for
styrene and phenol chains became the two largest aromatic outlets.

## Integration with refining {claim: engineering_principle}
Refinery-petrochemical integration exchanges streams in both directions: naphtha and
LPG to the cracker, pyrolysis gasoline and hydrogen back to the refinery. Integrated
sites are more efficient but couple the two plants' availability, so an outage in one
propagates to the other.

## Scale and single-train economics {claim: engineering_principle}
Petrochemical economics reward large single trains, which means a single compressor,
cracker furnace set or extruder can carry an entire site's output. This drives extreme
attention to the reliability of a small number of critical machines - typically the
cracked gas compressor, the propylene refrigeration compressor and the extruder drive.

## Polymer processing {claim: historical_fact}
Polymerisation moved from high-pressure autoclaves to low-pressure catalytic processes,
then to gas-phase and slurry-loop reactors, and finally to metallocene and
single-site catalysts from the 1990s that allow properties to be tuned precisely.

## Fouling and coking {claim: engineering_principle}
Cracking furnaces coke on the inside of the radiant coils and must be decoked on a
cycle measured in weeks. Managing run length between decokes, and tube metal temperature
as the limit, is the core operating discipline of an olefins plant.

## Current practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Operators pursue crude-to-chemicals configurations, mechanical recycling and pyrolysis
of waste plastics as feed, electrified cracking furnace concepts, and
condition monitoring on the machine train that determines site availability.
