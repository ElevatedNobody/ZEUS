## Factory legislation {claim: historical_fact}
Industrial safety regulation began with nineteenth-century factory acts limiting working
hours and requiring machine guarding. India's Factories Act of 1948 remains the primary
occupational safety statute for Indian manufacturing, with state factory inspectorates
enforcing it.

## Institutional milestones {claim: historical_fact}
The United States established OSHA under the Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970.
The United Kingdom passed the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act in 1974, creating the
Health and Safety Executive and establishing the goal-setting, duty-holder approach that
influenced regulation worldwide.

## Environmental regulation {claim: historical_fact}
Air and water pollution control law expanded rapidly from the 1960s and 1970s. In India,
the Water Act of 1974, the Air Act of 1981 and the Environment (Protection) Act of 1986 -
the last passed in the aftermath of Bhopal - established the pollution control board
structure and consent regime under which industrial sites operate.

## Industry bodies {claim: historical_fact}
India's Oil Industry Safety Directorate was set up in 1986 under the Ministry of
Petroleum and Natural Gas and publishes the OISD standards that govern much of Indian
oil and gas safety practice. The Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation, whose
lineage runs back to the Department of Explosives established in 1898, licenses storage
and handling of petroleum and explosives.

## From injury rates to risk {claim: historical_fact}
HSE performance was originally measured almost entirely by lost time injury frequency.
The Texas City investigations in 2005-2007 established publicly what specialists had
argued for years: personal injury rate is not a measure of major accident risk, and
using it as one is itself a hazard.

## The hierarchy of controls {claim: engineering_principle}
Controls are ranked: eliminate, substitute, engineer, administrate, then protect with
PPE. Personal protective equipment is the last line, not the first, and a control scheme
that relies mainly on PPE and procedures is weak by construction.

## Occupational exposure {claim: engineering_principle}
Chronic exposure hazards - benzene, hydrogen sulphide, noise, silica, asbestos in older
plants, heat stress - are managed by exposure monitoring against occupational limits,
engineering control of the source, and health surveillance, not by awareness alone.

## Hydrogen sulphide {claim: engineering_principle}
H2S is the characteristic acute toxic hazard of oil and gas work: heavier than air,
rapidly fatal at high concentration, and treacherous because it deadens the sense of
smell. Fixed and personal detection, area classification and rescue planning are
standard controls wherever sour service exists.

## Behaviour and systems {claim: engineering_principle}
Behavioural safety programmes help with slips, trips and hand injuries. They do not
substitute for barrier management in process safety, because the failures that cause
major accidents are usually organisational and technical rather than momentary
inattention.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current HSE practice integrates occupational safety, process safety, environment and
occupational health into a single management system - typically ISO 45001 and ISO 14001
aligned - with leading indicators, incident classification, contractor management, and
independent auditing.
