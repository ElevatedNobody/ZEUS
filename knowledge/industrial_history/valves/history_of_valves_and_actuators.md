## Early valves {claim: historical_fact}
Plug and gate type valves date to antiquity for water supply. Industrial valve
development followed the steam engine: Denis Papin's steam digester of 1679 carried the
first recognisable safety valve, a weighted lever that lifted at a set pressure.

## Materials and seat technology {claim: historical_fact}
Modern quarter-turn ball valves became practical for general service after the
introduction of PTFE, discovered by Roy Plunkett in 1938, which provided a low-friction
resilient seat. Metal-seated ball and triple-offset butterfly valves later extended
quarter-turn valves into high temperature and abrasive service.

## Control valves {claim: historical_fact}
Diaphragm-actuated globe control valves with pneumatic positioners became the standard
final control element in the mid twentieth century. Digital valve controllers from the
1990s added diagnostics: valve signature, friction trend, travel deviation and stroke
counting.

## Relief devices {claim: historical_fact}
API 520 and 521 codified the sizing and selection of pressure relieving devices and the
determination of relief loads, and API 526 standardised flanged steel relief valve
dimensions, making relief valves interchangeable between manufacturers.

## Valve selection principles {claim: engineering_principle}
Gate and ball valves are for isolation, not throttling; globe valves throttle; check
valves prevent reverse flow; butterfly valves suit large low-pressure duty. Using an
isolation valve for control wears the seat and produces poor control at the same time.

## Control valve sizing and authority {claim: engineering_principle}
A control valve must take enough of the system pressure drop to have authority over
flow. Oversized valves operate nearly closed, cutting seats and producing unstable
control at low flow - one of the most frequent causes of poor loop performance found in
plant surveys.

## Fail-safe position {claim: engineering_principle}
Every actuated valve has a defined action on loss of air or power - fail open, fail
closed, or fail in place - selected from the process hazard, not from convenience. The
fail position is a protection assumption and must be verified during commissioning and
after any actuator change.

## Passing valves {claim: engineering_principle}
A valve that passes when closed defeats isolation. Passing isolation valves are a
frequent contributor to incidents during maintenance, which is why positive isolation -
double block and bleed, spading or physical disconnection - is required for
break-containment work rather than reliance on a single closed valve.

## Relief valve testing {claim: modern_best_practice}
Relief valves are tested on a defined interval, with as-found set pressure recorded
before any adjustment. A high as-found lift pressure is integrity information: it means
the device would have opened late in the period just ended, and the interval or the
service conditions need review.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current practice uses digital valve controllers to trend friction and travel on critical
loops, partial stroke testing on emergency shutdown valves to extend proof test
intervals under IEC 61511, and fugitive emission certified packing to meet emissions
requirements.
