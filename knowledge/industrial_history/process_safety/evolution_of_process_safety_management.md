## Safety before process safety {claim: historical_fact}
Industrial safety originally meant occupational safety: guarding machines, preventing
falls, reducing lost-time injuries. It took a series of major accidents to establish
that a plant can have an excellent injury record and still be close to a catastrophic
loss of containment, because the two are controlled by different barriers.

## Flixborough, 1974 {claim: historical_fact}
A temporary bypass assembly installed between reactors at the Nypro plant at
Flixborough, England, failed on 1 June 1974, releasing cyclohexane that formed a vapour
cloud and exploded, killing 28 people. The inquiry highlighted uncontrolled plant
modification and led directly to the discipline of management of change.

## Seveso, 1976 {claim: historical_fact}
A runaway reaction at Icmesa near Seveso, Italy on 10 July 1976 released dioxin over a
populated area. No one was killed immediately, but the contamination and evacuation
drove the European Seveso Directive of 1982, revised as Seveso II in 1996 and Seveso III
in 2012, which requires operators of major-hazard sites to demonstrate control.

## Bhopal, 1984 {claim: historical_fact}
On the night of 2-3 December 1984, water entered a methyl isocyanate storage tank at the
Union Carbide India Limited plant in Bhopal. The resulting runaway reaction released
toxic gas over the surrounding city, killing thousands. Multiple protection layers -
refrigeration, scrubber, flare - were out of service or inadequate. Bhopal remains the
deadliest industrial accident on record and changed regulation worldwide.

## The institutional response {claim: historical_fact}
The American Institute of Chemical Engineers founded the Center for Chemical Process
Safety in 1985 in direct response to Bhopal. In the United States, OSHA issued the
Process Safety Management standard 29 CFR 1910.119 in 1992 and the EPA the Risk
Management Program rule in 1996.

## Piper Alpha, 1988 {claim: historical_fact}
The Piper Alpha platform in the North Sea was destroyed on 6 July 1988 with the loss of
167 lives. A condensate pump was restarted while its relief valve was removed for
maintenance, with the permit-to-work handover failing to communicate that fact. Lord
Cullen's inquiry produced 106 recommendations, moved the United Kingdom to a
goal-setting safety case regime, and made permit to work and emergency shutdown design
central topics of offshore safety.

## Texas City, 2005 {claim: historical_fact}
Fifteen workers died at BP's Texas City refinery on 23 March 2005 when a raffinate
splitter was overfilled during start-up and hydrocarbon discharged from an atmospheric
blowdown stack. The US Chemical Safety Board and the independent Baker Panel found
failures of instrumentation, procedures, siting of temporary trailers, fatigue
management and - most influentially - the use of personal injury rates as a proxy for
process safety performance.

## Buncefield, 2005 {claim: historical_fact}
A gasoline storage tank at Buncefield, England overfilled on 11 December 2005, forming
a large vapour cloud that exploded. The investigation drove major changes to overfill
protection, independent high-level trips and bunding for large fuel storage sites.

## Deepwater Horizon, 2010 {claim: historical_fact}
The blowout on 20 April 2010 killed 11 people and caused a prolonged subsea release.
Investigations again identified misread pressure tests, weak barrier management and
organisational factors rather than a single equipment defect.

## The core model {claim: engineering_principle}
Process safety is barrier management. Hazards are controlled by independent layers -
process design, basic control, alarms with operator response, safety instrumented
systems, relief devices, containment, and emergency response. Incidents happen when
multiple layers are degraded simultaneously, which is why the condition of each barrier
must be known, not assumed.

## Leading and lagging indicators {claim: engineering_principle}
Lagging indicators count events that already happened; leading indicators measure
barrier health - overdue proof tests, safety-critical elements out of service, backlog
of high-priority integrity work, deferred inspections. A safe-looking lagging record
with poor leading indicators is the pattern that preceded several of the accidents above.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current practice combines hazard identification (HAZOP, HAZID), layer of protection
analysis, formal management of change, pre-startup safety review, mechanical integrity
programmes with defined integrity operating windows, competency assurance, and process
safety event reporting to a recognised classification such as API RP 754.
