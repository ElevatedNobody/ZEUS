## From steam to electric drive trains {claim: historical_fact}
Process plant machinery started as reciprocating steam-driven pumps and compressors.
Electric motors and steam turbines progressively replaced them, and by the mid twentieth
century the standard refinery train was a centrifugal machine driven by an induction
motor or, for large services, a condensing or back-pressure steam turbine.

## Standardisation {claim: historical_fact}
The American Petroleum Institute machinery standards - notably API 610 for centrifugal
pumps, API 617 for axial and centrifugal compressors, API 618 for reciprocating
compressors, API 611 and 612 for steam turbines and API 682 for shaft sealing systems -
codified decades of refinery experience into purchase specifications that dominate the
industry.

## Rotor dynamics {claim: historical_fact}
Understanding of critical speeds, whirl and bearing stability developed through the
twentieth century, allowing high-speed machines to run safely above their first
critical speed. Machinery protection systems with proximity probes and API 670
compliance became standard for large critical machines.

## Sealing technology {claim: historical_fact}
Packed glands gave way to mechanical seals, then to cartridge seals with engineered
flush plans, and for gas service to dry gas seals from the 1970s and 1980s. Sealing is
the single most common source of both fugitive emissions and unplanned pump outages.

## Why rotating equipment dominates maintenance load {claim: engineering_principle}
Rotating machines have moving parts in contact, and therefore wear; they are the
principal energy consumers; and their failure usually stops a process. Consequently they
absorb the majority of maintenance labour and are the primary target of condition
monitoring.

## The dominant failure modes {claim: engineering_principle}
Across industry, the recurring mechanical causes are unbalance, misalignment, resonance,
looseness, bearing degradation, lubrication failure, cavitation and operation away from
the best efficiency point. Vibration spectra distinguish them: unbalance at one times
running speed, misalignment typically at two times with axial component, bearing defects
at their characteristic frequencies, and cavitation as broadband noise.

## Operating point matters {claim: engineering_principle}
A centrifugal pump run far below its best efficiency point suffers recirculation, higher
radial load and shortened seal and bearing life. A large proportion of chronic pump
failures are cured by correcting the hydraulic duty - trimming an impeller, changing a
control scheme - rather than by better repair.

## Compressor surge {claim: engineering_principle}
A centrifugal compressor driven below its minimum stable flow surges: flow reverses
periodically, imposing severe thrust and thermal cycling. Anti-surge control with a
recycle valve is a protection function, and its response time is what determines whether
a trip damages the machine.

## Vibration acceptance criteria {claim: engineering_principle}
ISO standards for evaluation of machine vibration by measurement on non-rotating parts
(the ISO 10816 series, now succeeded by ISO 20816) group machines by size and support
stiffness and define zones from newly commissioned condition through to unacceptable.
Zones are guidance for setting alarms, not a substitute for trending an individual
machine against its own baseline.

## Precision maintenance {claim: modern_best_practice}
Laser shaft alignment, field balancing to a defined residual, controlled bolt tightening,
correct lubricant with contamination control, and a documented baseline vibration
signature after every intervention are the practices that separate plants with long mean
time between failures from those without.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Critical machines carry online vibration and temperature monitoring feeding a machinery
protection system and a condition monitoring historian; balance of plant is covered by
route-based portable data collection; and both feed the same reliability analysis.
