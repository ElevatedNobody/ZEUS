## Ancient and early machines {claim: historical_fact}
The Archimedes screw and the piston pump are ancient. Denis Papin described a
centrifugal pump with straight vanes in the 1680s, but the centrifugal pump only became
dominant when high-speed electric and steam drives made its characteristics attractive
in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

## Industrial standardisation {claim: historical_fact}
API 610 established the refinery standard for centrifugal pumps - centre-line mounting
for hot service, heavy baseplates, defined bearing life and nozzle load limits. ANSI
B73 covers lighter chemical duty pumps. API 682 standardised mechanical seal systems and
flush plans.

## Sealless pumps {claim: historical_fact}
Magnetic drive and canned motor pumps removed the shaft seal entirely for hazardous and
toxic service, trading seal maintenance for sensitivity to dry running and to solids.

## Pump types and their duty {claim: engineering_principle}
Centrifugal pumps suit high flow at moderate head with clean or lightly laden liquids.
Positive displacement pumps - reciprocating, gear, screw, diaphragm - suit high
viscosity, low flow, high pressure or metered dosing, and must never be discharged
against a closed valve without relief protection.

## The pump curve {claim: engineering_principle}
A centrifugal pump follows a head-flow curve set by its impeller and speed; the system
imposes a resistance curve; the machine runs where they cross. Changing a valve position
moves the system curve, not the pump curve. Affinity laws relate flow to speed, head to
speed squared and power to speed cubed - which is why speed control saves so much energy
compared with throttling.

## NPSH and cavitation {claim: engineering_principle}
Net positive suction head available must exceed that required with margin. When it does
not, vapour bubbles form and collapse in the impeller eye: noise, vibration, loss of
head and pitting damage. Most cavitation is a suction-system problem - level, strainer
fouling, temperature rise, or a line resistance that was never checked at the actual
operating condition.

## Operation away from best efficiency point {claim: engineering_principle}
Running far from BEP increases radial load, shaft deflection and internal recirculation,
shortening seal and bearing life. A pump that fails repeatedly should be checked against
its actual duty before its components are blamed.

## Minimum flow protection {claim: engineering_principle}
Below minimum continuous stable flow a centrifugal pump overheats and recirculates.
Minimum flow protection - a recycle line with an orifice or automatic valve - is a
design requirement for pumps that can be blocked in, not an optional extra.

## Common failure modes {claim: engineering_principle}
Seal failure, bearing failure, cavitation damage, dry running, misalignment and pipe
strain account for the large majority of centrifugal pump failures. Pipe strain and
misalignment are installation defects and are prevented at the bolt-up stage, not by
better spares.

## Monitoring {claim: modern_best_practice}
Routine practice is vibration trending at the bearing housings, bearing and seal chamber
temperature, discharge pressure and flow against the expected curve, seal pot level and
pressure for pressurised seal plans, and oil analysis on larger machines.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Reliability programmes for pumps combine precision installation, correct seal flush plan
selection under API 682, minimum flow verification, variable speed drives where duty
varies, and root cause analysis of repeat failures rather than repeated like-for-like
repair.
