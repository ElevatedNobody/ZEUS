## Early understanding {claim: historical_fact}
Humphry Davy demonstrated cathodic protection of copper-sheathed ships' hulls with
sacrificial iron in 1824, the first engineered corrosion control. Faraday's work in the
1830s established the electrochemical basis that explains why it works.

## Corrosion as an industrial discipline {claim: historical_fact}
NACE - the National Association of Corrosion Engineers - was founded in 1943 by
pipeline corrosion engineers in the United States and became the principal source of
corrosion standards, later merging into AMPP. Its standards on materials for sour
service and on cathodic protection remain central to oil and gas practice.

## The cost of corrosion {claim: historical_fact}
A NACE study for the United States Federal Highway Administration, published in 2002,
estimated the direct cost of corrosion in the United States at about 276 billion dollars
per year, roughly 3.1 percent of GDP. The global IMPACT study published in 2016
estimated about 2.5 trillion dollars per year worldwide, around 3.4 percent of global
GDP. Both studies concluded that a large fraction is avoidable with available practice.

## The electrochemical basis {claim: engineering_principle}
Aqueous corrosion requires an anode, a cathode, a metallic path and an electrolyte.
Removing any one stops it. Every practical control - coatings, inhibitors, cathodic
protection, material change, dehydration - works by removing or interrupting one of the
four.

## Uniform versus localised attack {claim: engineering_principle}
Uniform thinning is predictable and manageable by corrosion allowance. Localised
attack - pitting, crevice corrosion, stress corrosion cracking, microbiologically
influenced corrosion - can penetrate a wall while average thickness looks acceptable,
which is why thickness surveys alone are not a complete integrity programme.

## Refinery high-temperature mechanisms {claim: engineering_principle}
Above about 260 degrees Celsius, sulphidation of carbon and low-alloy steels becomes
significant and is controlled by chromium content; the Couper-Gorman curves relate rate
to temperature and sulphur. Naphthenic acid corrosion attacks high-velocity areas in
crude and vacuum units processing acidic crude. High temperature hydrogen attack
degrades steels in hydrogen service and is bounded by the Nelson curves in API 941.

## Aqueous refinery mechanisms {claim: engineering_principle}
Overhead systems suffer hydrochloric acid corrosion at the water dew point, controlled
by desalting, caustic injection and neutralising amines. Sour water systems suffer
ammonium bisulphide corrosion at high velocity. Amine units suffer amine stress
corrosion cracking in unstressed-relieved carbon steel.

## Corrosion under insulation {claim: engineering_principle}
CUI is one of the most costly and most missed mechanisms. Water enters damaged
insulation, is held against the steel and corrodes it out of sight, most aggressively in
cyclic and intermittently wet temperature ranges. Control is by coating selection,
sealing and targeted insulation removal at supports, penetrations and low points.

## Material selection and corrosion allowance {claim: engineering_principle}
A corrosion allowance is a deliberate sacrificial thickness based on an expected uniform
rate over a design life. It is not protection against localised attack, and it expires:
equipment operating past its design life on consumed allowance requires a fitness for
service assessment, not an assumption.

## Monitoring {claim: modern_best_practice}
Practical corrosion monitoring combines process chemistry sampling (chlorides, pH, iron
counts, ammonium bisulphide concentration), corrosion coupons and electrical resistance
probes, permanently installed ultrasonic sensors at high-rate CMLs, and periodic
inspection - each measuring something the others cannot.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current practice manages corrosion by corrosion loops with a documented damage mechanism
review, integrity operating windows on the process variables that drive each mechanism,
and inspection plans built from API 571, 580 and 581 rather than from fixed intervals.
