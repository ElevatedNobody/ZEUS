## Works fire brigades {claim: historical_fact}
Large industrial sites have maintained their own fire crews since the nineteenth
century, because public services could not reach a hydrocarbon fire fast enough or with
the right media. Foam, dry chemical and large-capacity water supply became standard site
equipment.

## Learning from disasters {claim: historical_fact}
The response failures at Piper Alpha in 1988 - the loss of the control room, the failure
to shut down neighbouring platforms still feeding the fire, and the absence of a
survivable evacuation route - reshaped offshore emergency design toward temporary refuge,
protected escape routes and defined shutdown authority.

## Emergency planning regulation {claim: historical_fact}
The Seveso Directives in Europe, the EPA Risk Management Program in the United States
and, in India, the Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules of 1989
require major-hazard sites to prepare on-site emergency plans and to support district
off-site planning.

## Incident command {claim: historical_fact}
Structured incident command systems, developed originally for wildland firefighting in
the United States in the 1970s, were adopted by industry because they scale from a small
event to a multi-agency response with clear single-point control.

## Emergency shutdown philosophy {claim: engineering_principle}
Emergency shutdown reduces inventory and isolates sections. Its design must consider
what the shutdown itself creates: blocked-in hot liquid, loss of cooling on a running
reactor, or a flare load larger than the system can take. A shutdown that creates a new
hazard is not a safe state.

## Depressurisation and blowdown {claim: engineering_principle}
Rapid depressurisation reduces the driving force for a leak and the risk of vessel
failure in a fire, but generates a large flare load and can cause low-temperature
embrittlement in the depressurised equipment. Blowdown rate is designed against both.

## Fire and gas detection {claim: engineering_principle}
Detector coverage is designed for the credible leak scenarios and dispersion behaviour,
not placed uniformly. Voting arrangements balance spurious trips against failure to
detect, and detector availability is a proof-tested safety function.

## Human factors in an emergency {claim: engineering_principle}
Under stress people follow trained routines and struggle with novel diagnosis. This is
why muster procedures, roll call, radio discipline and pre-written scenario response are
drilled: they replace diagnosis with recognition at the moment when diagnosis is least
reliable.

## Mutual aid {claim: modern_best_practice}
Industrial estates commonly operate mutual aid schemes so that neighbouring sites share
foam stocks, appliances and trained crews. Effectiveness depends on compatible equipment,
joint exercises and pre-agreed command interfaces.

## Modern practice {claim: modern_best_practice}
Current practice includes scenario-based emergency plans tied to the site's own hazard
studies, regular full-scale and tabletop exercises with recorded learning, defined
temporary refuge and escape provision, and post-incident investigation feeding both
emergency plans and design.
