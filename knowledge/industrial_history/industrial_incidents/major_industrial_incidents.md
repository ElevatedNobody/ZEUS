## Purpose of this record {claim: engineering_principle}
Incident history is engineering data. Each entry below states what happened and what the
investigation concluded. These are general industry facts, not statements about any
particular plant, and must never be cited as if they came from a user's own document.

## Grover Shoe Factory boiler explosion, 1905 {claim: historical_fact}
A boiler explosion in Brockton, Massachusetts on 20 March 1905 destroyed the factory and
killed 58 people. It led to Massachusetts boiler law and to the first ASME Boiler and
Pressure Vessel Code in 1914-1915.

## Texas City ship explosion, 1947 {claim: historical_fact}
Ammonium nitrate cargo aboard the SS Grandcamp detonated at Texas City on 16 April 1947,
killing several hundred people. It remains a reference case for the hazards of bulk
oxidiser storage and for emergency response to a fire involving unknown cargo.

## Flixborough, 1974 {claim: historical_fact}
A temporary 20-inch bypass between reactors failed at Nypro's caprolactam plant on 1 June
1974. About 28 people died in the vapour cloud explosion. Cause: an unassessed plant
modification designed without engineering analysis. Legacy: management of change.

## Seveso, 1976 {claim: historical_fact}
A runaway reaction released dioxin over inhabited areas near Seveso, Italy on 10 July
1976. Legacy: the European Seveso Directives on control of major accident hazards.

## Three Mile Island, 1979 {claim: historical_fact}
A partial core meltdown at Unit 2 in Pennsylvania on 28 March 1979 followed a stuck-open
relief valve that operators misdiagnosed because of ambiguous indication. Legacy: human
factors engineering of control rooms and alarm systems across all process industries.

## Bhopal, 1984 {claim: historical_fact}
Water ingress into a methyl isocyanate tank at Union Carbide India Limited on the night
of 2-3 December 1984 caused a runaway reaction and toxic release over the city, killing
thousands. Multiple protective systems were unavailable. Legacy: worldwide major-hazard
regulation, the founding of CCPS in 1985, and India's Environment (Protection) Act 1986.

## Chernobyl, 1986 {claim: historical_fact}
A reactor test conducted outside its safe operating envelope, on a reactor design with a
positive void coefficient, destroyed Unit 4 on 26 April 1986. Legacy: safety culture as
an explicit concept, and the principle that procedures and design must both be
respected.

## Piper Alpha, 1988 {claim: historical_fact}
167 died on 6 July 1988 in the North Sea after a condensate pump was restarted with its
relief valve removed, following a permit-to-work handover failure. Legacy: the Cullen
Report, the safety case regime, and permit-to-work discipline as a core process safety
element.

## Longford, 1998 {claim: historical_fact}
An explosion at Esso's Longford gas plant in Victoria, Australia on 25 September 1998
killed two workers and cut gas supply to the state for two weeks. The Royal Commission
found that plant engineers had been relocated away from the site, weakening the
organisation's ability to interpret abnormal conditions.

## Texas City refinery, 2005 {claim: historical_fact}
Fifteen died and many were injured on 23 March 2005 when a raffinate splitter was
overfilled during start-up and hydrocarbon discharged from an atmospheric blowdown stack
and ignited. Legacy: elimination of atmospheric blowdown for flammables, occupied
building siting rules, fatigue management, and the distinction between personal and
process safety metrics.

## Buncefield, 2005 {claim: historical_fact}
A gasoline tank overfilled on 11 December 2005 in Hertfordshire, England, creating a
vapour cloud that exploded. Legacy: independent high-level overfill protection and
secondary and tertiary containment requirements for fuel depots.

## Deepwater Horizon, 2010 {claim: historical_fact}
A well control failure on 20 April 2010 killed 11 workers and caused a prolonged subsea
oil release. Investigations cited misinterpreted negative pressure tests, weak barrier
verification and organisational factors.

## The common pattern {claim: engineering_principle}
Across these events the recurring elements are: an unassessed change, a degraded or
bypassed protection layer, ambiguous or misread indication, procedures not matching the
plant, and organisational pressure that normalised the deviation. Very few were caused
by a single unforeseeable equipment defect.
